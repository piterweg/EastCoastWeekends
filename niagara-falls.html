Niagara Falls in a weekend

The one trip here that needs two nights: the train is slow, the falls deserve a full day, and the border adds time.

Why go

Plan around the journey

The Maple Leaf runs once a day from New York and takes about nine hours. Flying to Buffalo and driving forty minutes is faster but adds airport time.

Two nights is the minimum: one to arrive, one full day at the falls, one to travel back. Stay on the Canadian side for the view or the New York side for the state park; bring a passport if you plan to cross.

What to do

What to book

The falls are free; the experiences are timed and seasonal.

Boat

Maid of the Mist

The classic close-up. Seasonal, usually April to November; book a timed slot and expect to get wet.

Cave of the Winds

Niagara Falls State Park

Free to walk. The gorge trail and Prospect Point are the best free views.

The plan

Two nights, one full day

Arrive, see the falls, sleep, repeat.

01 Day one

Arrive

Train or flight to Buffalo, transfer to Niagara Falls, check in and walk to the falls for sunset. Early dinner near the park.

  • Buffalo or Niagara Falls station — Transfer by taxi or bus
  • Prospect Point — First view of the falls
02 Day two

The falls properly

Maid of the Mist in the morning, Cave of the Winds after lunch, then the gorge trail. Evening free.

  • Maid of the Mist — State park dock
  • Cave of the Winds — Goat Island
  • Gorge trail — Below Prospect Point

What to know before you go

◆

Do not attempt it as a day trip

Nine hours each way leaves no time at the falls. Two nights is the minimum.

Questions

__FAQ_TITLE__

Can I do it as a day trip?

Not comfortably. The train is about nine hours each way, so two nights is the realistic minimum.

Train or fly?

The train is scenic but slow. Flying to Buffalo is faster overall but adds airport time and transfers.

Which side is better?

The Canadian side has the classic panoramic view; the New York side has the state park and Cave of the Winds. A passport lets you do both.
