Boston in a weekend

Three and a half hours by train, then a compact city you can cross on foot: the Freedom Trail, Beacon Hill and the North End.

Why go

A city built for walking

Boston's historic core is small. The Freedom Trail connects the main sights in a single line, and the North End is the reward at the end of it.

Stay in Back Bay or near Boston Common. The train arrives at South Station and the T gets you anywhere else. Two nights make the weekend relaxed; one night means choosing between the trail and a museum.

The plan

Two days in Boston

One day on the trail, one day on the water and in a museum.

01 Saturday

The Freedom Trail

Start at Boston Common, follow the trail through the Granary and Paul Revere's house, and finish with lunch in the North End. Afternoon at Quincy Market and the waterfront.

  • Boston Common — Park Street
  • Freedom Trail — Red-brick line
  • North End lunch — Hanover Street
02 Sunday

Beacon Hill and the MFA

Morning walk in Beacon Hill and along Charles Street, then the Museum of Fine Arts or the Isabella Stewart Gardner, depending on the weather.

  • Beacon Hill — Charles/MGH
  • Museum of Fine Arts — Green Line E
  • South Station — Amtrak home

Questions

__FAQ_TITLE__

Is one night enough?

It is tight. One night lets you do the Freedom Trail and one neighbourhood; two nights add a museum and a slower Sunday.

Do I need the T?

Mostly no. The historic core is walkable; the T is useful for the museum district and the airport.

When is the best time?

Late spring and early autumn. Summer is busy; winter is cold but the museums are quiet.
